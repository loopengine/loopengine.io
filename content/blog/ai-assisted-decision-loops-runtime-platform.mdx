Enterprise AI stopped being “chat in a sidebar” the moment models could call APIs, read spreadsheets, and propose changes in systems of record. That shift creates a new requirement: **runtime governance** for operational decisions — not another workflow diagram.

Boss Loops is an **open governed operational runtime platform** for AI-assisted enterprise systems. It connects **Providers** (intelligence), **Channels** (human coordination), and **Integrations** (systems of record) through **decision loops** — with deterministic **guards**, **evidence**, and **learning** on every transition.

This post reframes how to think about AI-assisted operations: runtime first, workflows second, chatbots never.

---

## Boss Loops is a runtime platform — not a workflow engine

**Workflows define the path.** Temporal, Salesforce Flow, and n8n coordinate durable steps, retries, and timers.

**Loops govern the transitions.** Boss Loops decides whether a state change may commit — who may act, under which policy, with what evidence — before side effects land in CRM, ERP, or marketing systems.

That distinction matters for RevOps, ABM, and platform teams shipping AI into production:

| Concern | Workflow engine | Boss Loops runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary question | What runs next? | May this transition commit? |
| Policy | Often external or implicit | **Guards** in the runtime |
| Audit | Reconstructed from logs | **Evidence** at commit time |
| AI role | Step inside a flow | **Provider** inside governance boundaries |

Boss Loops is **not** a replacement for Temporal or Salesforce. It is the **governance substrate** AI-assisted operations were missing.

## Three layers — not one “integrations” bucket

A common mistake is treating Slack, OpenAI, and Salesforce as the same kind of connection. They are not.

### Providers — intelligence systems

**Providers** generate intelligence: analyze, recommend, classify, predict, draft.

Examples: OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Perplexity Sonar.

- Providers **do not** approve deals in Slack.
- Providers **do not** write to CRM.
- Providers **do not** bypass guards.

### Channels — human coordination surfaces

**Channels** coordinate humans: request approvals, surface decisions, escalate operators.

Examples: Slack, Microsoft Teams, email, comments in Google Docs, messenger routing via OpenClaw.

- Channels **are not** systems of record.
- Channels **are not** governance (that is loops + guards).

### Integrations — systems of record

**Integrations** execute operational actions after policy passes: persist state, trigger workflows, apply writes.

Examples: Salesforce, PagerDuty, Jira, Google Workspace apply paths, Postgres, HTTP APIs.

- Integrations **are not** LLM APIs.
- Integrations **are not** human chat UIs.

```text
Providers          → intelligence in
        ↓
Decision loops + Guards   → governance (Boss Loops)
        ↓
Channels           → human coordination
        ↓
Integrations       → operational execution
        ↓
Evidence + learning
```

**Channels are not integrations. Providers are not workflow systems. Integrations are not governance.**

## Operational AI needs runtime governance

AI can now operate across enterprise systems. APIs and MCP expose operational surfaces faster than governance models matured.

Three forces make a **runtime layer** mandatory:

1. **Models propose; they should not commit.** A Provider may recommend a discount or qualify a lead — guards and human Channels decide.
2. **Humans still own authority.** Regulated and revenue-critical decisions need attributed actors, not anonymous chat threads.
3. **Evidence must be structural.** “Show me the prompt” is not enough — you need transition-level evidence tied to business objects.

Boss Loops governs **transitions between operational states** — not generic “tasks” or “jobs.”

MCP and tool gateways are connectivity. They are not a substitute for deciding whether an operational state change is allowed. Boss Loops sits at that decision boundary; Commerce Gateway and registry patterns handle controlled tool access **under** the same governance model ([docs](https://loopengine.io/docs/integrations/commerce-gateway)).

## Why dual-surface operations matter

The strongest GTM patterns split **where work happens** from **where decisions happen**:

| Surface | Role | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Work** | Context and artifacts | Google Docs proposal, Sheets pricing grid |
| **Decision** | Approve / reject / escalate | Slack interactive message |
| **Governance** | Policy and audit | decision loop instance |
| **Execution** | System of record | Salesforce stage, staged sheet apply |

**Work happens inside operational tools. Decisions happen inside channels. Governance happens inside loops. Evidence connects everything.**

### Google Docs + Slack

An LLM drafts proposal language in a Doc (Provider + work surface). Brand and legal approve in Slack (Channel). Only after a governed transition does the customer-visible version publish and Salesforce advance (Integration). Doc hashes and approver IDs live in **evidence**.

### Google Sheets + pricing exceptions

A rep stages a discount row (work surface). Finance approves in Slack (Channel). **Apply** to the sheet and CRM sync run as Integration steps — never directly from the model.

These patterns are documented as narrative examples on [loopengine.io](https://loopengine.io/docs/examples/dual-surface-docs-slack); managed connectors ship via [Boss Loops Cloud](https://loopengine.io/docs/cloud/api-reference) where documented — we do not claim full OSS parity with every hosted connector today.

## ABM and RevOps: operational-runtime examples

Workflow-centric stories (“automate the sequence”) miss the governance story revenue teams need.

### SDR qualification loop

- **Provider:** scores inbound lead against ICP.
- **Guards:** confidence threshold, required fields.
- **Channel:** Slack — SDR approve / reject.
- **Integration:** CRM stage update.
- **Learning:** compare predicted fit vs closed-won.

### Proposal approval loop

- **Provider:** drafts sections from opportunity context.
- **Guards:** margin floor, compliance keywords.
- **Channel:** Slack deal-desk + Doc comments for context.
- **Integration:** Doc publish + opportunity stage.

### Campaign approval loop

- **Provider:** generates outbound copy.
- **Guards:** brand voice + compliance blocklist.
- **Channel:** marketing + legal in Slack.
- **Integration:** MAP/SEP activates sends post-approval.

### Pricing exception loop

- **Provider:** optional band recommendation.
- **Guards:** margin floor, `human-only` apply.
- **Channel:** finance controller in Slack.
- **Integration:** staged Sheets apply + ERP/CRM sync.

Each example is a **decision loop** with operational evidence — not a chatbot that “does RevOps.”

## Temporal, Salesforce, and the execution layer

Keep durable execution where it belongs.

**Temporal** (or similar) should run long-running processes: retries, timers, sagas.

**Boss Loops** should govern checkpoints: may this shipment release, may this discount apply, may this campaign send?

```text
AI / Provider proposes
        ↓
Boss Loops transition (guards + evidence)
        ↓
Temporal activity executes approved side effects
```

Salesforce Flow can update records **after** a loop instance reaches an approved state — Flow executes; loops authorize.

We document this composition pattern on [Workflow + Loop](https://loopengine.io/docs/examples/workflow-plus-loop) without claiming a single shipped “Temporal adapter” product.

## Runtime learning — not just logs

**Evidence** is captured when the transition is evaluated: inputs, guard outcomes, actor attribution, Provider metadata (model id, prompt hash where applicable).

When loops complete, **learning signals** compare predicted vs actual outcomes — fuel for improving guards, thresholds, and playbooks. That is operational learning on governed decisions, not dashboard vanity metrics on token counts.

## Self-host runtime vs hosted cloud

**Today:** run `@loop-engine/sdk` in your infrastructure — local-first, embeddable, governance-native.
